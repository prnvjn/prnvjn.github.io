Introduction

I completed Professor Predict’s AI Literacy Lab to understand how large language models learn patterns, generate responses, and handle questions that need reliable evidence.

Artifact Description

I completed all four stages of the exercise. Here’s what I took from each part of it.

  1. STAGE 1 · COMPLETED

    How language models respond

    I learned how next-token prediction draws on patterns in training data and how post-training shapes the responses a model gives.

  2. STAGE 2 · COMPLETED

    Recognizing hallucinations

    The fictional Velmora question showed me how a model can generate a plausible answer without a reliable source.

  3. STAGE 3 · COMPLETED

    Training data, privacy, and tools

    I learned to distinguish training data from private information and to recognize when an external tool is needed.

  4. STAGE 4 · COMPLETED

    Choosing when to verify

    I identified drafting cover letters and explaining familiar algorithms as useful tasks. Legal advice, current information, and exact calculations call for additional verification.

Artifact-Specific Value Proposition

This exercise shows how I evaluate AI responses and decide when a task needs an external tool or a reliable source.

Customization for Audience

I used examples from writing, software, and everyday research so engineering teams, recruiters, and collaborators can see where I’d use AI and how I’d check its work.

Reflection

A confident AI response isn't necessarily a correct one. AI is most useful when we understand its limitations and verify important information.
Read my full reflection

This exercise helped me understand how large language models learn patterns and generate responses without necessarily verifying their accuracy. The fictional Velmora question showed me how an AI could produce convincing but incorrect information.

I was particularly surprised by the distinction between private information and data used to train AI models. I also learned that LLMs work well for familiar tasks like drafting cover letters and explaining algorithms, but require additional verification for legal advice, current information, and exact calculations.

Going forward, I’ll verify important information and use external tools or reliable sources when a task calls for them.

Feedback and Revisions

The Velmora exercise prompted me to look for evidence behind a fluent answer. I used that example to make my reflection more specific about when and how I’ll verify AI output.
