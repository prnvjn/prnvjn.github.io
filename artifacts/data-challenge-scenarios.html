ARTIFACT 04 / RESPONSIBLE MACHINE LEARNING / CASE STUDY

Data Challenge Scenarios.

Auditing sentiment labels, evaluating a hiring pilot, and protecting patient data in a federated learning study.

CREATOR
Pranav Jain · individual exercise
CONTEXT
AI-guided practice
FORMAT
Case study and reflection
EVIDENCE
Three fictional scenarios

Introduction

I chose this exercise for my portfolio because it shows how I evaluate the data and assumptions behind a machine learning system. I worked through three fictional cases covering sentiment labels, résumé screening, and collaboration between hospitals.

Artifact Description

I proposed a decision for each case and refined it in response to follow-up questions from an AI coach. The case notes below document my proposed workflows and validation plans.

Case Notes

ReviewPulse · Audit candidate sentiment labels

I proposed a repeatable cleaning pipeline that started with one-star and five-star reviews as candidate negative and positive examples. To test those labels, I proposed randomly sampling 100 reviews from each confidently labeled group, hiding the ratings and pipeline labels, and asking reviewers to judge the text. I would report the confirmation and uncertainty rates for each group, keeping uncertain cases in the denominator. The audit would measure agreement for the two extreme ratings.

TalentBridge · Test whether the hiring model transfers

I began by comparing hiring needs across deployment settings. The follow-up brought out a risk even when roles are similar. Qualified applicants may use local credentials the model rarely saw. I proposed using local reviewers’ job criteria to examine where the model and reviewers disagreed, then running a limited pilot with a third reviewer to assess those disagreements.

Riverbend and Lakeside · Collaborate without moving raw records

I proposed a federated learning pilot with shared diagnosis definitions, code mappings checked at both hospitals, and patient records kept on-site. Each hospital would evaluate the shared model on recent held-out records against its own local baseline, checking missed diagnoses, false alarms, and calibration across sites and patient groups. I included access controls, audit logs, retention rules, and incident response. If either hospital saw more missed diagnoses, I would investigate before expanding.

Artifact-Specific Value Proposition

This exercise shows how I turn concerns about labels, representation, and privacy into questions a team can test. It also shows how I use independent review to check decisions before relying on a model at scale.

Customization for Audience

I organized the cases around decisions and validation methods so instructors, technical recruiters, and applied ML teams can follow what I proposed and what evidence I would collect.

Reflection

This exercise showed me where my intuition was well grounded and where I needed to be more specific. The highest and lowest ratings gave me a starting point for candidate labels. Hiding those ratings from reviewers was the more important decision, along with reporting uncertainty and keeping those cases in the count.

Bias and privacy were harder. A model can miss credentials that matter locally, even for similar roles. A limited pilot with local human review and a third reviewer could help explain where the model’s choices diverge from reviewers’ recommendations. In the hospital case, federated learning with local validation fit the collaboration, and I identified that model updates could still leak information in a small two-site pilot.

I can outline safeguards, but I still need to specify what evidence would halt a pilot. For the hiring case, I would agree upfront on how large a gap in rejection rates among equally qualified applicants across credential groups would trigger a pause, and who would make that call.

Feedback and Revisions

The coach asked what one hospital could infer if it knew its own update and saw the exact combined update. I revised my plan so the hospitals would receive neither exact per-round aggregates nor checkpoints that revealed them. I proposed a trusted coordinator, an upfront privacy budget, and release of a model with patient-level differential privacy, subject to checking whether it still improved on local baselines, including for smaller patient groups.
